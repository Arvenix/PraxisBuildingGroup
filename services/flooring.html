Flooring

Flooring Installation Across Southeast Michigan

Most flooring problems are subfloor problems. The finish material is usually innocent.

Request a Consultation Call 833-637-8466

What Goes Under the Floor Decides How It Performs

Flooring failures follow a short list of causes: a subfloor that was not flat enough for the material, moisture that was never measured, an expansion gap that was not left, or a transition between two materials that was not planned. Almost none of them are the fault of the flooring itself.

Different materials have different tolerances. Large format tile is unforgiving of a subfloor that is out of plane. Solid hardwood needs acclimation and moisture content within a range. Luxury vinyl plank tolerates moisture well but telegraphs unevenness. Each of those is a measurable condition, not a guess.

Praxis Building Group measures before installing, and corrects the substrate rather than hoping the material bridges it.

Typical Project Scope

A typical flooring scope includes:

  • Assessment of the existing floor structure, subfloor and any moisture source
  • Moisture testing of concrete slabs and wood subfloors where the material requires it
  • Removal and disposal of existing flooring and underlayment
  • Subfloor repair: replacing damaged sheathing, refastening squeaks, correcting deflection
  • Flattening with self leveling compound or patching where the material requires it
  • Underlayment appropriate to the material and location
  • Vapor retarder over slabs where applicable
  • Installation of hardwood, engineered wood, luxury vinyl plank, tile or carpet
  • Tile setting with correct mortar coverage, movement joints and layout
  • Transitions between materials and to adjoining rooms
  • Base shoe, quarter round or base trim reinstallation
  • Stair treads, risers and nosing where included
  • Final cleaning and protection

Not every project includes every item. Scope is written for your specific house and stated plainly before pricing.

The Praxis Building Group Approach

Measure the substrate first

Flatness gets checked against the tolerance the specific material requires, not against a general impression. A floor that looks fine can be well outside the specification for large format tile. Correcting it costs less than a cracked tile field.

Moisture gets tested, not assumed

Concrete slabs, particularly below grade, move moisture continuously. Wood subfloors in older homes can be well outside the acceptable range for a hardwood installation. Testing is quick and inexpensive relative to the cost of a failed floor.

Layout is planned before the first board

Where the run starts, how it relates to the longest sight line, where cuts land at doorways, and how the pattern resolves at a hearth or island all get decided in advance. This is most of what separates a professional installation visually.

Transitions are designed

Where two materials meet, or where a floor meets an existing floor of different thickness, the detail should be intentional. Height differences, door clearance and threshold placement are all part of the plan rather than improvised at the end.

Construction Considerations

Below grade and on slab installations

Solid hardwood is not appropriate directly over a below grade slab in Michigan. Engineered wood, luxury vinyl plank and tile all are, with the correct preparation. Getting this right at selection avoids a floor that cups or gaps seasonally.

Radiant heat

Floors over radiant heat require materials rated for it and careful attention to maximum surface temperature and acclimation. Not every product is compatible, and manufacturer warranties are explicit about it.

Old subfloors and squeaks

A floor replacement is the only convenient opportunity to fix squeaks by refastening the subfloor to the joists. It is inexpensive while the floor is open and effectively impossible afterward.

Continuity across a renovation

In a multi room or whole home project, running the same flooring continuously changes how the house reads and removes transitions. Whether that is possible depends on existing floor heights, which is worth confirming before the material is selected.

Planning This Project

Flooring is usually installed late in a project, after painting and before final trim, but it is selected early because it affects door clearances, cabinet toe kick heights, appliance fit and transition planning.

In an occupied home, flooring is one of the more disruptive trades because it requires rooms to be empty. Coordinating furniture movement in advance keeps a one day installation from becoming a three day one.

Questions

Questions About Flooring

Can I put hardwood in my basement?

Not solid hardwood. A below grade slab moves moisture even when it appears dry, and solid wood responds by cupping and gapping. Engineered wood, luxury vinyl plank and tile are all appropriate below grade with the correct preparation and vapor management, and several of them are visually indistinguishable from solid wood at floor level.

Do you have to remove the old flooring?

Usually yes. Installing over existing flooring raises the floor height, which affects doors, appliances and transitions, and it hides subfloor problems that should be corrected. There are cases where an existing sound layer is an acceptable substrate, and Praxis Building Group will say so when that applies rather than defaulting to it.

Why does large format tile need such a flat floor?

Because a large rigid tile cannot follow a curve. Where the substrate dips, the tile bridges it, leaving a void under part of the tile and a lippage difference at the edge. That void is where cracks start. The larger the tile, the tighter the flatness tolerance the manufacturer specifies.

Can you match my existing hardwood?

Sometimes closely, rarely exactly. Species, cut, board width, stain and decades of light exposure all factor in. Where a match matters, options include refinishing the existing and new floor together as one field, or making a deliberate transition at a doorway. Praxis Building Group will show you both before proceeding.
