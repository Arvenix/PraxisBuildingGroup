Plumbing

Plumbing Within Remodeling and Construction

Supply lines are flexible. Drains are not. That single fact drives most layout decisions in a kitchen or bathroom.

Request a Consultation Call 833-637-8466

Drainage Sets the Constraints

Water can be routed almost anywhere. A supply line is small, it is under pressure, and it can turn corners freely. Drainage is the opposite: it runs on gravity, it needs a specific slope, it needs venting to work at all, and the pipe is large enough that where it can run is limited by joist direction and how much of a joist can be drilled.

This is why moving a sink a few feet is straightforward and moving a toilet across a room may not be. It is also why the plumbing conversation should happen while the layout is still being decided rather than after it is committed.

Praxis Building Group coordinates plumbing within the project so those constraints are known at the planning stage, when they are still cheap to work around.

Typical Project Scope

Plumbing work on our projects can include:

  • Evaluation of existing supply piping, drainage, venting and water heating
  • Supply line replacement where galvanized or failing material is present
  • Rough in for kitchen, bathroom, laundry, wet bar and utility fixtures
  • Drain, waste and vent modification within code requirements
  • Fixture relocation, including toilet drain relocation where feasible
  • Shower valve and tub filler rough in with correct depth and blocking
  • Water heater replacement or relocation as part of the project
  • Gas line work for ranges, dryers and appliances where included
  • Ejector pump systems for below grade fixtures where gravity drainage is not available
  • Exterior hose bibs, shutoffs and freeze protection
  • Fixture setting and trim out
  • Pressure testing and inspection coordination

Not every project includes every item. Scope is written for your specific house and stated plainly before pricing.

The Praxis Building Group Approach

Layout gets tested against the drain

Before a kitchen or bathroom layout is finalized, we confirm where drainage can actually run: joist direction, available depth for slope, vent path, and what is below the floor. A layout that requires an impossible drain run is better caught on paper.

Access is preserved

Shutoffs, cleanouts and connections need to remain reachable after the project is finished. Tiling over a cleanout or framing a soffit around a valve creates a problem for whoever needs it next, which is often the homeowner at an inconvenient moment.

Old supply lines get evaluated honestly

Galvanized steel supply piping corrodes from the inside and restricts flow long before it leaks. In an open wall during a renovation, replacing it is inexpensive. Leaving it and finishing over it means the next replacement involves the new finishes.

Blocking and depth before drywall

Shower valve depth, wall mounted faucet backing, grab bar blocking and fixture heights all get set during rough in against the actual finished wall thickness. Getting this wrong is only discovered at trim out, when it is expensive.

Construction Considerations

Moving a toilet

Relocating a toilet means relocating a large drain with required slope, within the constraints of joist direction and permitted boring. It is often possible, sometimes only in one direction, and occasionally not without structural work. This is worth resolving before the bathroom layout is committed.

Below grade fixtures

A basement bathroom, wet bar or laundry may sit below the elevation of the sanitary line leaving the house. Where it does, an ejector pump system is the solution. It works well, it requires a pit and venting, and it needs to be in the plan rather than added at the end.

Venting in older homes

Drain venting arrangements in older homes are frequently non compliant by current standards. Once a bathroom or kitchen is opened under permit, the work generally has to be brought current. That is a real scope item and belongs in the estimate.

Water heater capacity and location

Adding bathrooms or fixtures raises demand. A renovation is the practical time to evaluate whether the existing water heater is adequate, and whether relocating it frees space that the project needs.

Planning This Project

Fixture selection affects rough in. A wall mounted faucet, a freestanding tub with a floor mounted filler, or a specific shower valve all require rough in decisions that cannot be deferred to trim out. Select fixtures before rough in, not after.

Questions

Questions About Plumbing

Can you move my toilet to the other side of the bathroom?

Often, but not always, and the answer comes from what is below the floor. A toilet drain is large and needs consistent slope to the stack, which means it has to run parallel to the joists or within the limits of what can be bored through them. Praxis Building Group determines the feasible options before the layout is finalized rather than discovering the constraint at demolition.

Should I replace my galvanized supply lines?

If the walls are open as part of the project, yes. Galvanized piping corrodes internally, restricting flow long before it leaks, and it will need replacing eventually. Doing it while the wall is already open costs a fraction of doing it after new tile and drywall are in place.

What is an ejector pump and do I need one?

An ejector pump lifts waste from fixtures located below the elevation of the sanitary line leaving the house, which is common for basement bathrooms and laundry. Whether you need one depends on the depth of your existing sanitary line. If gravity drainage is available, that is simpler and preferable. We determine which applies before the basement layout is set.

Does Praxis Building Group do plumbing repairs?

Plumbing is coordinated within remodeling, renovation and construction projects rather than offered as standalone repair service. If your project includes plumbing work, we handle it under the project contract. For an isolated leak or a failed water heater with no other work involved, a plumbing contractor is the right call.
