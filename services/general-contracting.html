Project Management

Project Management and Trade Coordination

We are a remodeling and building company first. When a project needs someone to own the schedule, the sequence and the outcome, we take that role so you do not have to.

What You Are Actually Buying

Homeowners sometimes look at a managed project's price and see a markup on trade work they could hire directly. That is one way to look at it. The other is to ask what happens on a Tuesday when the framing is not ready for the electrician, the tile arrived short, and the inspector wants something changed before drywall.

On a self managed project, that is the homeowner's problem, and every one of those events costs days. On a contracted project it is the contractor's problem, and it is handled without the schedule collapsing.

What a managed project gives you is a defined scope, a sequence that works, trades that show up in the right order, permits and inspections handled, and one company accountable when something does not go to plan. On a multi trade project, that is most of the value.

Typical Project Scope

Project management on a Praxis Building Group project covers:

  • Scope development, with inclusions and exclusions written down
  • Realistic budgeting, including allowances set at meaningful amounts
  • Drawings and engineering coordination where the project requires them
  • Permit application and coordination with the local building department
  • Trade selection, contracting and scheduling
  • Material specification, procurement and lead time management
  • Construction schedule development and daily site coordination
  • Site protection, dust containment and safety
  • Inspection scheduling and code compliance
  • Change order documentation and pricing before work proceeds
  • Quality review at each stage rather than only at the end
  • Punch list development and closeout
  • Final inspections, occupancy where applicable, and handover

Not every project includes every item. Scope is written for your specific house and stated plainly before pricing.

The Praxis Building Group Approach

Scope is written before it is priced

A number attached to a vague description is not an estimate, it is a guess that will be corrected later at the homeowner's expense. We write down what is included and what is not, so that the price refers to something specific and can be compared fairly against another contractor's price for the same work.

Sequencing is the deliverable

The order of operations determines whether a project runs. What has to be open, inspected and closed before the next trade can start. Where the long lead items sit. When selections are actually needed. We build that sequence in preconstruction and manages against it.

Change orders are priced in advance

Changes happen. What should not happen is work performed and priced afterward. Whether the change originates with the homeowner or with a condition found in the house, it gets documented and priced before it proceeds.

One point of accountability

When two trades disagree about whose work caused a problem, a self managed homeowner has to arbitrate it. On our projects, that is not the homeowner's conversation. The contract is with one company and so is the responsibility.

Construction Considerations

Acting as your general contractor, or managing on your behalf

Acting as the general contractor, Praxis Building Group contracts with the trades and carries the project. Under a construction management arrangement, we run the project on the owner's behalf while the owner holds the trade contracts. Both are legitimate structures and they suit different situations, particularly for investors and owners with existing trade relationships.

When you do not need a managed project

A single trade project with no dependencies does not need one. Replacing a water heater, painting a room, or installing carpet is a direct hire. Project management earns its cost when multiple trades have to be sequenced and inspected in relation to each other.

Investors and repeat renovation work

Owners renovating multiple properties often want a repeatable scope and predictable turnaround rather than a bespoke design process. We have production construction experience and can structure work that way, with defined specifications applied across properties.

Working with your own architect or designer

Where a homeowner already has drawings or a designer, we work from them. Constructability review early in that relationship is useful, because it is far cheaper to resolve a detail on paper than after it has been framed.

Planning This Project

The most useful thing a homeowner can bring to a first conversation is a clear statement of what they want to change and what outcome matters most: budget, schedule, level of finish, or minimum disruption. Those trade against each other, and knowing which one leads shapes every recommendation.

Comparing contractor prices only works when the scopes match. A lower price for a scope that omits electrical upgrades, permit costs or realistic allowances is not a lower price. Praxis Building Group will help you read what a competing scope actually includes.

Questions

Questions About Project Management

Why not just hire the trades myself?

On a single trade job, that often makes sense. On a multi trade project, you become the scheduler: coordinating who is on site when, resolving conflicts between trades, managing inspections, chasing materials and absorbing the cost of every delay. That is a real job, and the cost of doing it badly usually exceeds the contractor's fee.

What is the difference between an estimate and a bid?

In practice, the words get used loosely, which is part of the problem. What matters is whether the number is attached to a written scope. A figure with no defined scope behind it cannot be compared to anything and will change. Praxis Building Group prices defined work.

Who pulls the permits?

We do, as part of the project. Homeowners are sometimes asked by other contractors to pull permits themselves as the owner, which shifts responsibility for the work onto the homeowner. That is worth understanding before agreeing to it with any contractor.

Can Praxis Building Group take over a project another contractor started?

Sometimes. It depends on what was done, whether it was permitted, and what condition it is in. An honest assessment comes first, because taking responsibility for someone else's incomplete work means being able to stand behind it. Praxis Building Group will look at it and give you a direct answer either way.
