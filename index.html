Why Praxis Building Group

Built on Experience. Focused on Results.

Choosing a contractor is mostly a bet on judgment: whether the company knows what it is looking at, and whether it can run the job once the walls are open.

01

Decades on job sites

We are built around long hands on experience across trades, project types, construction methods and architectural styles. That experience shows up early, in the questions asked before a scope is written.

02

Every trade under one contract

Framing, electrical, plumbing, drywall, flooring, cabinetry, roofing and finish work are coordinated by Praxis Building Group. You are not left holding a list of phone numbers and hoping the schedule lines up.

03

Production and custom experience

Production work teaches repeatable process and cost control. Custom work teaches problem solving on houses that do not follow a plan. We have worked both, and apply whichever the project needs.

04

Sequencing is most of the job

The difference between a project that runs and one that stalls is usually order of operations: what has to be open, inspected, closed and finished before the next trade can start. That is planned up front.

05

Remodeling through new construction

The same company that can reframe a load bearing wall in a 1940s house can build a new structure from footings up. That range matters when a project turns out to be bigger than it first looked.

06

Accountability that does not move

One contractor holds the schedule, the trades, the inspections and the punch list. When something needs an answer, there is one company to ask.

How a project runs

From First Walkthrough to Final Punch List

The same sequence applies whether the project is a single bathroom or a whole house. The depth of each stage is what changes.

  1. Consultation

    We walk the space, talk through what you want changed and look at what the house will actually allow.

  2. Scope and Planning

    Scope gets written down: what is included, what is not, and where the decisions with cost impact sit.

  3. Preconstruction

    Selections, permits, trade scheduling and material lead times are lined up before demolition starts.

  4. Construction

    Trades are sequenced and coordinated, inspections are scheduled, and the site is kept in working order.

  5. Review and Completion

    Work is reviewed against the scope, the punch list is closed out and the space is handed back finished.
